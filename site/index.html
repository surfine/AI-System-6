AI SYSTEM 6

Your words should
still sound like you.

AI System 6 is a writing desk for your browser and your Mac, built on the rules of a 1988 Macintosh. Your sources, questions, drafts, and finished file each have a place you can see. The model may help, but it never quietly takes the pen.

Three ways

There are three ways
to write with AI.

You ask it to smooth out a paragraph. What comes back really is smoother. You paste it in and send it. The next day you reread it, and you cannot find yourself in it.

  1. 01

    Write in a chat box

    The answer is right there, one copy away from your draft. From then on, its words and yours are the same words.

  2. 02

    Let it fill in your editor

    Suggestions grow inside your sentences. Some editors color the machine's words; change a few letters and they count as yours.

  3. 03

    Write at a desk

    The model gets a window of its own, and what it writes stays in that window. To reach your work, it has to be carried there by you.

The difference isn't how clever the model is.
It's whether its words pass through your hands.

One desk

Every window does one job.

Scroll, and the camera walks across a real screenshot of the desk.

  1. 01

    Searcher finds sources

    It brings back titles, sites, and snippets: doors, not answers. To read the original, it hands you to Reader.

  2. 02

    Scrapbook keeps only what you chose

    Every clip remembers where it came from. The name was already on the first Mac, in 1984.

  3. 03

    The model lives in ClioTalk

    It can talk, suggest, and draft. Every word it writes stays in this window first. Here no model is connected, and the rest of the desk works anyway.

  4. 04

    Your manuscript lives in TeachText

    Words arrive here only when you put them here. Save, clip, insert, export: each one is a key you press.

  5. 05

    Review Desk reads it back

    Before the work leaves, it points out shaky facts, loose structure, and the sentences that have started to sound like a model.

  6. 06

    What you bring in rides on a floppy

    PDFs, pages, and recordings go on the File Floppy. They are reference for this piece of work, and never quietly become part of the project.

The one rule

Nothing saves itself.

A model reply is still only a reply. It becomes part of the work only when you save, clip, insert, or export it.

  • Output arrives temporary

    You can read it, reject it, or decide where it belongs without changing the manuscript.

  • You choose the destination

    Question Sheet, Outline, the current section, TeachText, Scrapbook, or a new project file.

  • Sources keep their identity

    Searcher finds a source door. Reader opens the original. Scrapbook keeps only the passages you chose.

    Back from a Walk in Quick Draft accepts a pasted transcript and keeps spoken marks as verbatim quotations. Confirm to add it as File Floppy material; your manuscript stays as you wrote it.

  • The desk reports what happened

    Saved, searched, clipped, reviewed: the desk records these when they actually happen. A model does not get to say them for you.

He writes A Song of Ice and Fire in WordStar 4.0, on a DOS machine that isn't connected to the internet, because it does what a writing program should and nothing else. AI System 6 was built overnight in May 2026, its first design document opens with his words, and every feature since has a story of its own.

Forty years

The desk changes.
The objects keep their meaning.

System 6 gave this desk its grammar: things you can see, saving on purpose, one job per window. The same desk then wears seven eras of Mac design, from 1988 to 2026. Windows gain shadows, then gloss, then glass. A file is still a file. Save still means save.

    The other road

    A branch that became
    the trunk.

    While the Mac was still running System 7, NeXT, the company Steve Jobs started after leaving Apple, was building a different desk: gray, beveled, black title bars, applications lined up in a Dock down the right edge. The first web browser was written on one. At the end of 1996 Apple bought NeXT, and NeXTSTEP became the foundation of Mac OS X. The Aqua above grew out of it.

    AI System 6 wears it as a branch, not a stop on the line: the same files and the same rules, on the desk the modern Mac grew from. Choose it from the Special menu.

    System 7, Drawing Board, Tiger and Lion are also included as experimental appearances; the eight regular choices stay unchanged.

    Two floppies.

    Read Me

    Do I need to connect an AI model first?

    No. With no model connected, the desk still opens, writes, saves, and exports. When you want AI, connect one in Control Panel: LM Studio or Ollama on your own computer, DeepSeek, or any OpenAI-compatible service.

    What role does the AI play on this desk?

    A companion, not a partner: it keeps you company while you write, but it doesn't share the say over the work. In practice:

    • It asks; it doesn't answer for you. With the Question Sheet open, press Interview me in SideAsk and the model asks one question at a time, only what you alone can answer: what you saw, who this is for, what someone actually said. It suggests no wording, drafts nothing, praises nothing, and your answer goes into the sheet as you wrote it. In Quick Draft it doesn't decide your point, your stance, or your title either; a title can only appear as a suggestion.
    • You bring the bones; what it adds waits for your yes. In Section Drafts the model writes only the section you picked. It shows you the result first, and it goes into the draft only once you confirm, marked AI Assisted.
    • Your words are not paraphrased. Dictation Pad's Shape only breaks what you said into lines and paragraphs, and changes not one word; if it would have to, it does nothing and tells you why. Imported chats and venting count as material to write from, never as checked fact.
    • It helps you check the piece reaches people. Before it goes out, you can open your own draft in DocMap as a map, or use Listen to hear it read back one beat at a time, marking where a listener would get lost.
    Where does my writing live?

    Your projects stay in this browser, on this computer. A local model keeps requests on your machine; cloud requests follow the policy of the provider you choose. The server keeps no second project database, and your keys never go into project files, conversations, backups, or exports.

    What does it cost?

    Nothing. AI System 6 is free and MIT open source. If you use a cloud model, what you pay is between you and that provider.

    Does it work on a phone or an iPad?

    Yes, upright or sideways. On an iPhone you can add it to the Home Screen and open it like an app.

    How is the Mac app different?

    It is the same desk. The Mac beta brings everything it needs to run, so you download it and open it; the browser version needs no install at all.

    Why does it look like a 1988 Mac?

    Its manners come from WordStar, the program George R.R. Martin still writes on: it does the writing and nothing else. Its shape comes from System 6, in 1988, because a desk of things you can see is the clearest way to show what AI would otherwise hide: which conversation is temporary, what you kept, what the model can see. It is not made by Apple.
