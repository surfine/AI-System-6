# Claude-Pop for AI System 6: the brief

This folder makes a promotional music video for **AI System 6** entirely in code: an original song,
written, arranged, sung and mixed by programs in this folder, and a picture drawn frame by frame on a
canvas. Nothing is recorded, sampled from a record or generated by an outside music or video model.

Every agent working here reads this file first. It is the contract.

## 1. What we are imitating, and what we are not

**The form** comes from [amemiya02/pdoom-video-win95](https://github.com/amemiya02/pdoom-video-win95), a
music video for "I'm Upping My P(doom)" (its Claude-Pop version) that is one long screen recording of a
fictional 1995 PC. Study its method, not its code (it carries no licence; write everything here from
scratch). What makes it work:

- **Every lyric is a user-interface gag.** Each sung line becomes a dialog, a utility, a game, an error.
  The lyric is on screen verbatim, readable, in sync, inside the scene's own UI (typed into a field, the
  message of a dialog, a window title).
- **Something happens on every sung word**, and the big hits land on beats.
- **Characters are interface objects.** There, the mouse pointer was the human and the paperclip was the AI.
- **A running prop tracks the song's argument** (there, a P(doom) progress bar climbing at each hook; a
  doomsday clock in the taskbar).
- **The colour scheme follows the song's sections.**
- **Deadpan.** The operating system reports enormous things in the flat voice it uses for a full disk.
- **Hard pixels.** 640x360 canvas, upscaled 3x nearest-neighbour, faint scanlines; 1-bit text
  rasterisation; no antialiasing, no gradients (dither instead); motion in whole pixels; windows zoom open
  from outlines; dragged windows leave trails.
- **Every frame is a pure function of song time.** No state between frames, no `Math.random()`, no `Date`.

**The music**: "Claude-Pop". We could not find an authoritative definition of the term (the reference only
says it used "the Claude-Pop version" of its song). Our working definition, which is the house style here:
a bright, hooky, slightly uncanny pop song **written and produced by Claude**, sung by a voice that is
plainly a machine and knows it. Its DNA is the music in **One More Tune**, AI System 6's own quiz about
the songs Apple put in its ads (see §3). It should sound like it could score a 30-second product spot, and
it should make you smile on the first listen.

## 2. The product (the only claims the video may make)

Tagline: **1988 OBJECTS / 2026 INTELLIGENCE.** "A local-first writing desk where the AI never becomes your
voice." Live at `system6.aaronlau.me`.

- **The belief.** Your language, sources, judgment, feeling for the subject and sense of who the piece is
  for are the valuable parts. A model smooths them into competent, forgettable prose if it holds the pen;
  here it does not hold the pen.
- **AI output is temporary** until you save, clip, insert or export it. You say where a reply lands.
- **The route is the product:** Project Hard Disk → File Floppy → Question Sheet → Outline → Section
  Drafts → Manuscript (TeachText, read-only while drafting) → Review Desk → Project CD.
- **Review Desk** checks for drift into a model's voice: over-regular rhythm, generic summary language,
  flattened personal detail, press-release hedging. **Your roughness is not a defect.**
- Apps summoned onto the route: Searcher and Reader (live web), Time Machine (archived pages), File Floppy
  (imports with OCR and transcription), Scrapbook (evidence you chose to keep), DocMap, ClioTalk (chat is an
  app, not the whole computer), Note Pad, Dictionary, Writing Bell, Image Prompt Studio.
- **MultiFinder** keeps real working apps open together. Chat is an app. Not the whole computer.
- **Two floppies.** A release gate fails the build when the boot payload outgrows two 1.44 MB floppies:
  2,902,645 of 2,949,120 bytes. Heavy tools load lazily, from a third disk.
- **Bring your own model**: LM Studio, Ollama, DeepSeek. No model required to look around. Stateless
  server; projects live in your browser.
- **Twelve appearances**, one live desktop: 1988 System 6 · 1991 System 7 · 1999 Platinum · 2002 Aqua ·
  2005 Tiger · 2009 Snow Leopard · 2011 Lion · 2014 Yosemite · 2020 Big Sur · 2026 Liquid Glass, plus the
  branches 1995 NeXTSTEP and 1998 Drawing Board. The files and open windows stay put; the whole computer
  changes era around them.
- **Seven games**: DOOM, Micropolis (open-source SimCity), OpenTTD, Bonsai City, Rootline, Joyride,
  Plaintext. Compiled to WebAssembly, in windows beside the manuscript.
- **One More Tune**: a quiz about the music in Apple's ads. A white-label record with ten track positions;
  hear a few bars, name the ad; answer and the original opens while the whole desk wears the appearance of
  that year, then hands your appearance back.
- Every app has one shareable address, `/go/<app-id>`.

## 3. One More Tune's library: the sound we are descended from

The deck (`apps/desktop/data/one-more-tune-deck.json`, 108 cards) spans 1999-2025. The tracks that define
the lineage, and what to take from each (never their melodies, lyrics or recordings):

| Track | Ad | Take |
|---|---|---|
| Feist, "1234" (2007) | iPod nano | counting as a hook; banjo, handclaps, a horn line; a cappella-ish intimacy |
| Yael Naïm, "New Soul" (2008) | MacBook Air | wordless "la-la" hook; piano bounce; a voice alone at the start |
| Daft Punk, "Technologic" (2005) | iPod silhouettes | a robotic voice chanting a list of verbs over a fat loop |
| The Ting Tings, "Shut Up and Let Me Go" (2008) | iPod | chant, cowbell, claps, a playground taunt |
| CSS, "Music Is My Hot Hot Sex" (2008) | iPod touch | dance-punk bounce, deadpan delivery |
| Chairlift, "Bruises" (2008) | iPod nano | sweet synth duet, a little off-kilter |
| Caesars, "Jerk It Out" (2005) | iPod shuffle | one organ riff you can whistle |
| Propellerheads, "Take California" (2001) | first iPod | big-beat breaks, a spoken sample |
| Portugal. The Man, "Feel It Still" (2017) | iPad Pro | bass groove, falsetto |
| Marian Hill, "Down" (2017) | AirPods | sparse, finger snaps, a sax stab, space |
| Jungle, "GOOD TIMES" (2024) | keynote opener | disco strut, group vocals |
| Hark Madley, "Welcome To Joy" (2024) | Apple Intelligence | glossy, optimistic |

The rules of the genre: the hook arrives in the first five seconds; one signature riff on one
distinctive timbre; handclaps; counting, chanting or call-and-response; a lift you feel in the chest at
the chorus; done before it outstays its welcome.

## 4. Hard limits

- No Apple logo, no Happy Mac, no Apple startup chime (compose our own boot chord), no Apple product UI
  copied wholesale. AI System 6's own apps, names and appearances are ours to show.
- No melody, lyric or recording from any existing song. Song titles may appear as text only where One More
  Tune itself would show them, and sparingly.
- Product claims only from §2. Deadpan, dry, warm. Never "revolutionary", never "unleash".
- The singer is the AI. The words on screen are the writer's. That irony is the point: a synthetic voice
  singing that it will never become your voice.

## 5. The single source of truth: `music/score.json`

The song and the picture share one timeline because both are generated from one file. `music/song.py`
writes `music/score.json`; `music/build_song.py` renders `build/song.wav` from it; `music/export_timing.py`
writes `data/data.js` for the video. **Never hand-edit timings in the video; take them from `data/data.js`.**

```jsonc
{
  "title": "…", "bpm": 120, "key": "C major", "beatsPerBar": 4,
  "durationBeats": 280,                       // total length including the tail
  "sections": [ { "name": "intro", "startBeat": 0, "beats": 16, "label": "…" } ],
  "chords":   [ { "startBeat": 0, "beats": 4, "symbol": "Cmaj7", "notes": [48, 52, 55, 59] } ],
  "lines": [ {
      "id": "v1a", "section": "verse1", "voice": "lead",   // lead | chant | choir | spoken
      "text": "Exactly as shown on screen",
      "words": [ { "w": "hello", "notes": [ [69, 8.0, 0.5], [72, 8.5, 1.0] ] } ]   // [midi, startBeat, beats]
  } ],
  "parts": { … }   // arrangement data the producer needs: riff, bass, drum patterns per section, hits
}
```

Beats are absolute from the start of the song. Seconds = beat × 60 / bpm. `data/data.js` exposes
`LYRICS` (lines with `start`/`end` and per-word `start`/`end` in seconds), `SECTIONS`, `BEATS` (seconds of
every beat), `BARS`, `HITS` (named accents) and `DUR`.

## 6. The machinery

**Audio** (Python, run with `python3 -I`): Piper neural TTS (`.cache/voices/*.onnx`: `en_US-amy-medium`,
`en_US-lessac-medium`, `en_GB-jenny_dioco-medium`), sung by resynthesising each word with Praat PSOLA
(`parselmouth`: replace the pitch tier with the melody, the duration tier with the note lengths).
Prototype: `.cache/proto-sing.py`; it hit every pitch within 0.1 semitone and Whisper transcribed it word
for word. Instruments: FluidSynth with `/usr/share/sounds/sf2/FluidR3_GM.sf2`, plus anything synthesised
in numpy/scipy. QA: `faster_whisper` (`small.en`, pass a 16 kHz float32 numpy array, not a path), Praat
pitch tracking, `pyloudnorm`. `tools/setup.sh` installs all of it.

**Picture** (plain browser JavaScript, no bundler): `index.html` loads `data/data.js`, then `src/*.js`.
640x360 canvas. `render.mjs` drives headless Chromium and needs the page to define `window.READY`,
`renderFrame(t)` (returns PNG base64 without the data-URL prefix), `renderSheet(times, cols)`,
`renderCheck(t0, t1)` (returns `{frames, errors, avgMs, maxMs, maxAt}`) and `DUR`.

```bash
node render.mjs check 0 30               # every frame: exceptions and draw times
node render.mjs sheet 24 38 0.4 build/sheet.png    # contact sheet; open the PNG and look
node render.mjs still 25.6 build/still.png         # one frame at full 1080p
node render.mjs video                    # the whole thing, 1920x1080 60 fps, with build/song.wav
```

Pixel fonts for the System 6 look are in `../../system.css-reference/fonts/`: `ChiKareGo2` (Chicago, the
menu and title font), `FindersKeepers` (Geneva 9, small UI text), `monaco` (Monaco). Later eras may use
other faces, but text is always rasterised and thresholded so it stays hard-edged.
